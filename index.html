WHERE IT BEGAN

Before we knew these days would become memories.

There was a time when being together was simply part of every day. No planning. No calling. No wondering when we would meet next. We were just there.

We grew up together through ordinary days that never felt extraordinary at the time. The silly arguments. The teasing. The laughter. The little moments that nobody thought would matter years later.

But they do matter. More than I realised back then.

02 — OUR MEMORIES

A collection of little moments.

These photographs aren't just photographs. They are pieces of a childhood I sometimes wish I could visit again.

SOMETHING I DON'T SAY ENOUGH

I miss those days.

I miss how easy everything felt. We didn't have to make plans to spend time together. We didn't have to ask when we would meet again.

We were simply part of each other's everyday life.

And sometimes, that's what I miss the most. Not one particular day. Just us.

HOME

You are more than my sister.

When I say I miss you, sometimes I mean that I miss everything that came with you.

Mom. Dad. You. Me.

That little family. That little home. That little world we grew up in.

You are part of so many of my earliest memories. And I don't think I have ever properly thanked you for that.

"Some people are not just part of your life. They are part of the story that made you."

03 — A LETTER

Things I don't always know how to say.

RAKSHA BANDHAN

Dear Sravani,

I don't know if I have ever properly told you how much you mean to me.

I know I am not always good at expressing what I feel. Sometimes I want to say something, but I don't know how. Sometimes I just stay quiet.

But please never mistake that silence for not caring.

I love you. I respect you. I care about you. Probably much more than I know how to show.

I may tease you. I may annoy you. I may behave like everything is normal. But underneath all of that, you have always been someone I deeply care about.

I miss our childhood. I miss those stupid little fights. I miss laughing about things that probably weren't even funny. I miss having you around without thinking about distance, time, or how much life has changed.

I miss Mom. I miss Dad. I miss those days.

And I miss the feeling of having our whole little family together.

Sravani, you are not just my sister.

You are a part of my childhood. A part of my memories. A part of my family. A part of home.

I might not always say it. I might not always show it. But whenever you need me, I will be there.

No matter how much life changes.

With all my love and respect, Yaswanth

ONE LAST WISH

Keep smiling, Sravani.

I hope life gives you every happiness you deserve.

I hope your dreams become your reality.

I hope you always have reasons to smile.

And when life gets difficult, I hope you remember that you have a younger brother who will always care about you.

Happy Raksha Bandhan, Sravani.

— Yaswanth
